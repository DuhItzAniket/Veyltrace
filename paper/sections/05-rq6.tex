RQ6 replays the recorded \code{veyltrace:brief} runs of \code{re2-oracle-}\allowbreak\code{rq2-v1},\resnum{replay/recording}{tier1.n} runs in all.
No experiment configuration measures replay, so the rates are side results, one committed replay summary per site; a logged deviation of 22 November 2026 lets Table~\ref{tab:rq6} read them as it reads \code{summary.json}.
The recording laptop and the second laptop run Windows, and the CI job runs Linux; Table~\ref{tab:rq6} gives each site's commit.

\paragraph{Tier 1 (H6a)}
H6a holds if Tier-1 replay reproduces \code{verdict_hash} on every recorded run, on a second laptop and in CI.
Tier 1 re-runs each recorded run from its receipt, with the proposer reading the cassette (\S\ref{sec:receipts}).
Its rate is the share of runs whose \code{veyltrace replay} exits 0, as docs/04 §7 defines it.
We also count the runs whose recomputed \code{verdict_hash} equals the recorded one, whatever the exit code, which is H6a's literal wording.
On the recording laptop, \resnum{replay/recording}{tier1.k} of \resnum{replay/recording}{tier1.n} runs exit 0 (rate \resnum{replay/recording}{tier1.rate}, exact 95\% CI \resnum{replay/recording}{tier1.ci}).
On the second laptop, \resnum{replay/second-laptop}{tier1.k} of \resnum{replay/second-laptop}{tier1.n} runs exit 0 (\resnum{replay/second-laptop}{tier1.rate}, CI \resnum{replay/second-laptop}{tier1.ci}).
In CI, \resnum{replay/ci}{tier1.k} of \resnum{replay/ci}{tier1.n} runs exit 0 (\resnum{replay/ci}{tier1.rate}, CI \resnum{replay/ci}{tier1.ci}).
Two kinds of failure make up the gap.
First, the same OB run (seed 3) exits 6 on the second laptop and in CI: \resnum{replay/second-laptop}{tier1.outcomes.6} run on the second laptop and \resnum{replay/ci}{tier1.outcomes.6} in CI.
Exit 6 means that, of the hashes replay decides on (verdict, trace and card), only the card differs.
The diagnostic comparison reports \code{evidence.parquet} as the first divergent artefact for this run, and also for many runs that exit 0 on the second laptop and in CI, but for none on the recording laptop; no summary key holds that count (it is only in each site's per-run \code{replay.csv}), so we give none.
That run's recomputed \code{verdict_hash} equals the recorded one.
Second, on the second laptop \resnum{replay/second-laptop}{tier1.outcomes.timeout} TT run (seed 1) was killed at the bench timeout and produced no replayed hash.
The verdict is therefore reproduced on \resnum{replay/second-laptop}{tier1.k_verdict} of \resnum{replay/second-laptop}{tier1.n} runs on the second laptop and on \resnum{replay/ci}{tier1.k_verdict} of \resnum{replay/ci}{tier1.n} in CI.
No replay that finished produced a different \code{verdict_hash}.
Under the Tier-1 definition of docs/04 §7 (exit 0), H6a misses on the second laptop and in CI.
Under H6a's literal wording (\code{verdict_hash} reproduced), it holds in CI and misses on the second laptop, because of the timeout.
In both readings, the misses are a narrative that differed and a replay that did not finish; no replay that finished changed a verdict.

\paragraph{Tier 2 (H6b)}
H6b reports the Tier-2 regeneration match rate and sets no target.
Tier 2 regenerates a fixed, seeded sample of \resnum{replay/recording}{tier2.n} recorded \code{veyltrace:brief} runs with proposer seed 1, in a fixed hash order, with the model, and a run matches if its regenerated \code{proposals.json} is byte-identical to the recorded one.
A logged deviation of 22 November 2026, made before any replay ran, moved Tier 2 from a single-slot \code{llama-server} to the recording's own Ollama provider with the same model; \S\ref{sec:receipts} describes the pre-registered runtime.
No regeneration matched: \resnum{replay/recording}{tier2.k} of \resnum{replay/recording}{tier2.n} (rate \resnum{replay/recording}{tier2.rate}, exact 95\% CI \resnum{replay/recording}{tier2.ci}).
The regenerated verdicts did not carry the recorded \code{verdict_hash} either: \resnum{replay/recording}{tier2.k_verdict} of \resnum{replay/recording}{tier2.n} did.
On this setup, then, a verdict is reproducible from its recorded model output, not by running the model again.
