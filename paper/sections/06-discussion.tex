\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Where the verifier loses and where it abstains}
The verifier, not the slate, costs \sys its accuracy.
The true root is in the top five candidates almost always (\S\ref{sec:rq1}), and the fused ranker slate alone reaches an AC@1 of \resnum{re2-oracle-rq1-v1}{ob/causatrix:rrf/ac_at_1}, \resnum{re2-oracle-rq1-v1}{ss/causatrix:rrf/ac_at_1} and \resnum{re2-oracle-rq1-v1}{tt/causatrix:rrf/ac_at_1} on OB, SS and TT, against \resnum{re2-oracle-rq1-v1}{ob/veyltrace:off/ac_at_1}, \resnum{re2-oracle-rq1-v1}{ss/veyltrace:off/ac_at_1} and \resnum{re2-oracle-rq1-v1}{tt/veyltrace:off/ac_at_1} after verification (point values; no paired test is committed).
The telemetry ablation narrows the search.
With metrics alone, \code{veyltrace:off} scores \resnum{re2-metrics-only-v1}{ob/veyltrace:off/ac_at_1}, \resnum{re2-metrics-only-v1}{ss/veyltrace:off/ac_at_1} and \resnum{re2-metrics-only-v1}{tt/veyltrace:off/ac_at_1}, higher than with every source, and dropping logs alone raises SS AC@1 by \resnum{re2-oracle-rq1-v1}{ss/veyltrace:off/ac_at_1/difference/vs:re2-drop-logs-v1}.
On these point values, adding logs, traces and events to metrics lowers AC@1 on every RE2 system; no committed result holds an interval or a test for a cross-experiment difference.
The ablation removes a source from the whole pipeline, so the committed results do not say whether the loss arises in the rankers or in the verifier's trials; that is the first question for the next version.
The verified score also orders risk worse than the fused score (H3c), so the verifier's confidence is not yet a better basis for abstention than the fused score.

The no-fault answer does not work as fitted in v1.0.
Under the fit convention of docs/04 §12, no held-out normal window is declared \NOFAULT; under the literal reading of the protocol, the false-alarm rate falls to \resnum{rq3-intervals/re2-oracle-rq3-off-v1}{literal_tau_sensitivity.pooled.false_alarm_rate.estimate} (95\% CI \resnum{rq3-intervals/re2-oracle-rq3-off-v1}{literal_tau_sensitivity.pooled.false_alarm_rate.ci}), still above the 0.15 limit, and \NOFAULT is then wrongly declared on \resnum{rq3-intervals/re2-oracle-rq3-off-v1}{literal_tau_sensitivity.pooled.wrong_no_fault_rate.estimate} of fault cases, so both targets miss under that reading (a sensitivity analysis computed after the results).
Calibrated on OB and SS, the threshold does fire on TT, but with the same trade-off: false alarms on \resnum{re2-oracle-loso-off-v1}{tt/veyltrace:off/no_fault_rates.false_alarm_rate} of normal windows and wrong \NOFAULT on \resnum{re2-oracle-loso-off-v1}{tt/veyltrace:off/no_fault_rates.wrong_no_fault_rate} of fault cases.
The candidate sets themselves cover the root at the target rate within RE2, and stay close to it with OB or SS held out (\resnum{re2-oracle-loso-off-v1}{ob/veyltrace:off/coverage} and \resnum{re2-oracle-loso-off-v1}{ss/veyltrace:off/coverage}, with 95\% CIs \resnum{rq3-intervals/re2-oracle-loso-off-v1}{intervals.ob.coverage.ci} and \resnum{rq3-intervals/re2-oracle-loso-off-v1}{intervals.ss.coverage.ci} that contain 0.90), but not with TT held out (\resnum{re2-oracle-loso-off-v1}{tt/veyltrace:off/coverage}) or on Nezha after calibration on RE2: \resnum{re2-to-nezha-transfer-v1}{ob/veyltrace:off/coverage} and \resnum{re2-to-nezha-transfer-v1}{tt/veyltrace:off/coverage} on Nezha OB and TT.
Split-conformal coverage assumes that calibration and test cases are exchangeable; these results suggest that an operator should calibrate on the system being diagnosed.
We planned to examine where the verifier abstains by fault class, for instance on network faults, where the internal-before-external onset precedence can flip.
As a limit of v1.0, we report no fault-class breakdown, although docs/04 §10.6 asks for the network-fault cells: no table generated before the results-v1 freeze holds one, and the protocol takes every reported number from a summary file (docs/04 §10.2).
Readers can compute it from the released per-case outputs (\code{per_case.csv}).

\paragraph{What the LLM adds}
On RE2 with the oracle alert, the code-verified proposer adds no detectable accuracy (H2a; Holm-adjusted $p = $ \resnum{re2-oracle-rq2-v1}{pooled/veyltrace:brief-vs-veyltrace:off/ac_at_1/holm_p}) at a p95 cost above its 180\,s budget (\S\ref{sec:rq5}).
Its value, if any, is that verification bounds the harm of a misled model.
An unverified agent over the same proposals scores a higher AC@1 on clean inputs (\resnum{re2-oracle-rq2-v1}{ob/agent-only:brief/ac_at_1}, \resnum{re2-oracle-rq2-v1}{ss/agent-only:brief/ac_at_1} and \resnum{re2-oracle-rq2-v1}{tt/agent-only:brief/ac_at_1}, against \resnum{re2-oracle-rq2-v1}{ob/veyltrace:brief/ac_at_1}, \resnum{re2-oracle-rq2-v1}{ss/veyltrace:brief/ac_at_1} and \resnum{re2-oracle-rq2-v1}{tt/veyltrace:brief/ac_at_1}; exploratory, with no test).
With one planted decoy, its top-1 changes in \resnum{re2-oracle-planted-v1}{ob/agent-only:brief/decoy_top1_change}, \resnum{re2-oracle-planted-v1}{ss/agent-only:brief/decoy_top1_change} and \resnum{re2-oracle-planted-v1}{tt/agent-only:brief/decoy_top1_change} of runs and its AC@1 falls to \resnum{re2-oracle-planted-v1}{pooled/agent-only:brief/ac_at_1}, while the verified arm's top-1 changes in \resnum{re2-oracle-planted-v1}{ob/veyltrace:brief/decoy_top1_change}, \resnum{re2-oracle-planted-v1}{ss/veyltrace:brief/decoy_top1_change} and \resnum{re2-oracle-planted-v1}{tt/veyltrace:brief/decoy_top1_change} of runs and its pooled AC@1 is \resnum{re2-oracle-planted-v1}{pooled/veyltrace:brief/ac_at_1}.
The agent takes the model's order as its ranking and the decoy is placed first, so the decoy becomes its top-1 by construction; these flips show the placement of the decoy, not a measured weakness of the model.
The comparison shows only that the verifier does not follow the proposal order, at the price of the agent's accuracy on clean inputs; the verified arm's decoy rate still misses the 0.05 target on OB and SS in point estimate, and only on SS at the lower bound of its exact interval (H2b).
We cannot say through which predicates the LLM acts.
The per-predicate request counts are not in the committed results, and no committed file records how many proposals were admitted, accepted or rejected; both would need a new summary entry (docs/03 §13.3).
Three planned comparisons are also missing: the \code{investigate} mode was cut, a 9B against a 4B model was never configured, and the proposer ran only on RE2 with the oracle alert (\S\ref{sec:rq2}).

\paragraph{\sys as a verification tool for other agents}
Because the verifier evaluates every requested predicate for every candidate, it could serve other RCA agents as a check behind a tool interface such as MCP.
The MCP server moved to v1.1 (ADR \code{20261123-cl-}\allowbreak\code{explain-scope-cuts}), so we have no result for this use.
Our RQ2 results suggest what such a check would offer: a planted claim placed first did not become the verified top-1 by default, as it does for an agent that follows the model's order, although it still changed that top-1 more often than the 0.05 target on OB and SS; and on clean RE2 inputs the verified arm scored below the unverified agent, as \code{veyltrace:off} scores below the fused slate.
An agent that calls the verifier would therefore do better to treat a disagreement as a flag to inspect than as a correction.

\paragraph{The limits of synthetic decoys}
RCAEval-C's change logs are synthetic, and the true change is easy to spot: on OB a rule that names the latest change reaches AC@1 \resnum{rcaevalc-pi1-l0-v1}{ob/naive-change/ac_at_1} when a true change is present, and when only decoys are present, at 6 per hour, it ranks a decoy first in \resnum{rcaevalc-pi0-l6-v1}{ob/naive-change/decoy_ranked_first}, \resnum{rcaevalc-pi0-l6-v1}{ss/naive-change/decoy_ranked_first} and \resnum{rcaevalc-pi0-l6-v1}{tt/naive-change/decoy_ranked_first} of cases on OB, SS and TT.
\sys ranks a decoy first in \resnum{rcaevalc-pi0-l6-v1}{ob/veyltrace:off/decoy_ranked_first}, \resnum{rcaevalc-pi0-l6-v1}{ss/veyltrace:off/decoy_ranked_first} and \resnum{rcaevalc-pi0-l6-v1}{tt/veyltrace:off/decoy_ranked_first} of those cases, far less often than the naive rule, but a true change raises its AC@1 little (H4c).
The benchmark therefore tests how gullible the change channel is, not whether it separates a real faulty deploy from routine ones.
No v1.0 result uses real deploy, configuration or feature-flag events (ADR \code{20261124-as-}\allowbreak\code{observe-cuts-v1-1}).
The planted decoy proposals of RQ2 are synthetic in the same way: we plant one kind of false claim, and a real adversary or a misled model may produce others.
